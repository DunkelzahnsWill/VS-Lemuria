**Decisions.** Each is mine as the writer of this book, and each is open to the review thread and the owner before
it merges.

1. **What a feature is in Lemuria: a part of the mod with a folder of its own.** Nine are built: crystals, cradle,
   journal, sparkstone, storage, evocations, earmuffs, machines, mysteries. Why: the record has the Road Bible give
   "for each feature the owning thread and its folders", and puts "every file or folder that sits in no single
   feature's folders" on the shared list. Main is already cut that way, file for file: I checked all 327 files
   outside record/, and 296 fall in exactly one feature's folders and 31 are shared files, with none left over. The
   README, the code and the old notes use "feature" for these parts ("all of it in the `Machines` feature"), and R5's
   question names "feature folders" by these names. The patch gate of rule 7 tests "the folders of one feature"
   against "that feature's brief", which works only if a feature's brief describes what its folders hold.
   Why not the other way, a feature for each old step or part, one to a signed-off note, seven in all: none of those
   has folders of its own (three share Machines, and every old step from 1 on wrote into Journal). All code would
   then sit on the shared list, every patch to built code would be "out", and a patch would be judged against a
   brief that does not describe the folder it changes.
   What it costs: R3 has mason write the built features' briefs "from the signed-off note as built". With nine
   features and seven notes, mason cuts the notes by folder, so the book says which old steps' notes each brief
   draws on. I had spoken of "the step 1 brief (the duets)" before; that still holds, because step 1 has one feature.
2. **Features 10 to 27 are cut along the old roadmap's own seams,** each a part that can have a folder of its own.
   Why not one feature to a step: a brief for a whole old step would be very large, and the owner cut old steps 4
   and 5 into parts. Why not finer: every cut is a guess before the step is designed. These cuts are the least
   certain part of the book. A step's features can be cut again by a change to the book before its first brief
   (rule 4), and a number retired then is not used again.
3. **Step 7 lists balance, the multiplayer test and the release as work that is no feature.** Why: none has a folder
   or a brief of its own. Balance changes settled briefs, the test is the owner's play, and the release is the
   owner's alone (rule 4).
4. **The old roadmap's "gates" are kept as "aims in play", which hold nothing.** Why: R8 has the Road Bible take over
   the roadmap, and each of its steps had a gate in play. Under rule 7 a gate is the inquisitor's test, and one word
   must not mean both. Why not drop them: they are what the owner agreed each step should let a player do, and they
   feed the briefs' "what to try" lists.
5. **The concept is carried from the pre-production document's concept,** with the standing rules of the design
   gathered in one list (R9's one claims switch among them), and without the numbers that belong to single features.
   The old glossary, about seventy words, is not copied. The concept defines the words the whole mod stands on, and
   the rest stay in the old paper until a brief carries them. Why not copy it: most of its words belong to one
   feature and carry numbers that play has changed and will change again, and every change to the concept sends
   every thread back to read this book (rule 5).
6. **The prototype's terms name no proto folder** (R1). They say where the work a proto thread would have held now
   lies: on the shared list, under mason.
7. **The shared list.** Everything outside record/ is mason's (R5, R15). The old paper is named piece by piece, so
   that a new play-test folder or a new diary is not taken for old paper: each Claude Doc under its own title, because
   the code's comments cite the old notes by title (G10), and each file and folder by its path. The art and sound
   scripts in the project files are mason's tools, not old paper. Why: they are code that has to be run and changed
   to make new art, the precursor owns no code and changes nothing it keeps, and the owner's answer in pre-production
   keeps art scripts in the project files. What else lies beside the old paper is given by name as well. That part
   was rewritten for G4, and "Added at 04:57Z" below says how and why.
8. **The repo's first commit, pushed straight to main in old step 0, is named beside the Precursor steps.** Why:
   rule 7's third test looks at how each change reached main, and R14 speaks of merged PRs.
9. **My map is not in this PR.** My entries of 03:31Z and 03:54Z said the map would come with the Road Bible. Rule 5
   sends every change to a map to main at once by a record-only PR, and at a thread's first map the review thread
