**Why these words.** I drafted them at 06:47Z, for the case that the owner answered B, and the owner took them as
drafted. What each part is for, and why not another way:
1. **"such a change"** points back to the sentence before: a change to code on main that follows a change to its
   brief. So the new sentences open that one case and no other, and the other feature's brief is still changed first.
   G36 asked that B keep this.
2. **"once the change to the other feature's brief is merged"** keeps the order in a word nobody has to ask about. Why
   not "is changed": a later thread could ask whether a change that is drafted and not yet merged would do.
3. **"that the thread owns or has been handed"** follows the words of rule 7's gate on a patch, "one feature that its
   author owns or has been handed". It keeps the cast's bound: "Outside record/, a thread changes only the folders and
   files it owns under the Road Bible, or has been handed under these rules." In Lemuria mason owns every feature (R5,
   R15), so the bound shuts nothing out here. It is there for a project with more than one builder: by this sentence a
   feature's PR never reaches into another thread's folders.
4. **"may instead"** leaves way A as it was. A thread may still send such a change on its own, as a patch, and its
   verdict is then "out". Why not "rides": R23 asked whether such code may come in the feature's PR, and the answer B
   says that it may. Nothing in it forbids way A.
5. **The second sentence** is G36's second point. The owner merges a feature "from its description" (rule 3), so the
   description names what else the PR changes, and the merged change to each brief. G36 held that one sentence after
   the one it quotes would do. The description needed a second.
