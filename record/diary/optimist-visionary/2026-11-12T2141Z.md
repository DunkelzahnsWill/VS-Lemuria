**What I had to settle so that it works, and why so.** The review thread sent me nine points at 21:11Z, before I had
written a word, as what it will hold the draft against (G87). Most of what follows answers one of them.
- *One doer for each act (its point 3).* While a record thread is at hand it sends and wakes, "and the coordinator
  does not". With none at hand the coordinator does, "as these rules have it". The one way back is said outright: what
  the record thread sees that it cannot do in good time "it hands that one back: it tells the coordinator, and the
  coordinator makes it". Nowhere does the text say that either may act: with two doers a wake comes twice or not at
  all.
- *One clause, as version 4 did for the writing (its point 2).* The rules have the coordinator send an entry of
  rulings.md or a finding, or wake one of the two, in the cast, under "The record" and in rules 1, 6 and 7. I did not
  change each sentence. One clause covers them: "Wherever these rules have the coordinator send a thread an entry of
  rulings.md or of findings.md, tell a thread what such an entry says, or wake the review thread or the inquisitor,
  the record thread does it". The words "tell a thread what such an entry says" are there for rule 6, where "the
  coordinator tells the owning thread" of a rank that changed and "passes word of it to the janitor" after a handover.
  What rules 3, 4 and 5 have the coordinator send or tell a thread is none of these, and it stays the coordinator's
  own (above). Two sentences did have to change, because they would disagree with the clause: the one with "wakes no
  one", and rule 1's "No stop keeps a record thread from recording".
- *What sets off a wake (its point 4).* Most causes are told to the coordinator by a thread: that its PR is ready
  (rule 6), that a blocker is corrected, that it asks for a verdict (rule 7). The new text has such a thread tell "the
  record thread the same". Why not have the coordinator pass each one on: that costs the coordinator the very message
  that the change is to spare it. Why not have the thread tell the record thread alone, and the record thread tell the
  coordinator: the coordinator keeps the merge lists and should hear of a ready PR from its author, and the record
  thread's messages are the scarce ones (below). The pattern has stood in the cast since version 4, where a thread
  sends the owner's words "to the coordinator and to the record thread". What the coordinator alone knows of, "the
  owner's word to it or its own report that a step is done", it passes on. What the record thread reads on main
  itself, a map or the inquisitor's signed decision, it acts on, and it tells the coordinator. So the coordinator
  learns of every cause once, and rule 6's "has told the coordinator so" stays true as written.
- *Stops (rule 1).* The coordinator "still decides whom a stop holds". The entry names them, and the record thread's
  sending of that entry is what stops them, and later what lets them resume. Why not leave the stopping with the
  coordinator: each stopped thread would get two messages, one from each, for one act. Rule 1 already has stopped
  threads resume "when the coordinator sends them the entry", so the entry already carries the stop's end. And a stop
  goes first: "an entry that stops a thread goes first, and a wake next".
- *It still decides nothing (its point 5).* Whom an entry concerns "is the coordinator's to say, and the entry says
  it", and a finding names its owning thread. A doubt of the record thread's goes to the coordinator, as its doubt
  over what to write does under rule 1. A finding on a sending or a wake "is the coordinator's". That is how the
  review thread already takes a slip in the record thread's writing: G28 names as its owner "The coordinator, whose
  record thread wrote the entry".
- *The sixty messages an hour (its point 7).* Four things. A thread that can open the repo "may be sent an entry by
  its number alone", and reads it on main before it acts on it, so that a long finding costs one message and not
  several. The thread that tells of a cause tells the record thread itself. The record thread hands back what it
  cannot do in time. And the copy ends the reading of both books by message, which G83 counts at fifty messages each
  time.
- *The record shows it.* The record thread's diary "says what it has sent and whom it has woken". The review thread
  judges from the record (rule 6), and the cast has a returning coordinator send what was entered in its absence
  "unless the record shows it done".
- *The copy (its point 6), and what is Lemuria's alone (its point 9).* The rules say what holds for any project: who
  makes the copy and when, that it is named with its commit, that it is for reading only and no part of the record,
  that the repo stands where the two differ, and that reading the newest copy in full "is the reading of that book
  that these rules ask of" the coordinator. Where the copies lie is for the Road Bible's shared list to say, as it is
  for the old paper. So Lemuria's folder and file names stand in part 4 and nowhere in the rules. The words "a first
  copy as soon as there is none" have both copies made at this PR's merge, which is when rule 4 has every thread read
  the rules again. Part 4 keeps "the newest copy of each and no older one": a name with a commit id does not sort by
  age, and after a cut of its context the coordinator must not be able to pick an old copy.
- *Why two new paragraphs,* and not one longer one. The rules keep one line to a paragraph. So the record thread's
  paragraph shows in the diff with one sentence changed, and the new text can be handed to the sister project as it
  stands.
- *Why one PR for the rules and the book's row.* The coordinator told the owner that I write "both as one rules change
  for you to merge". Both go the same way under rule 4: the review thread's read and then the owner's merge. And the
  copy needs its place named on the day the rule takes force.
