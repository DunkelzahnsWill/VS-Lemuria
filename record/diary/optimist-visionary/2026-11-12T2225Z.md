# 2026-11-12 22:25Z: rules version 6, a second commit: what the review thread found before its report (PR #144)

**Why this entry.** The review thread read PR #144 at commit 2f5de71 and came to me at 22:16Z and 22:17Z with what it
found, before it reports (rule 6). Its first words: "The one clause sorts every sentence of version 5 in which the
coordinator sends, tells, wakes or passes, and the nine points of G87 are answered." It then names six things that
remain. For five of them I changed the text, by a script, in a second commit. This entry is that commit's own and
rides in PR #144 beside the entry of 21:41Z, which stays as it was written. Where the two differ, this one is the
later.

**The six points, and what I did with each.** Unless I say otherwise, the words in quotation marks are the review
thread's or the rules'.

1. *A wake that nobody misses.* "A thread tells the coordinator of a cause and forgets the record thread". Then the
   coordinator does not wake, the record thread knows of nothing, and nobody can see that a wake is missing. "The
   other way round as well: told alone, the record thread wakes, and the coordinator never hears that the PR is
   ready." And its cure: "If the record thread told the coordinator of each wake it makes, both would show."
   - *It is right, and my entry of 21:41Z is wrong in one sentence.* That entry says: "So the coordinator learns of
     every cause once". That holds only while every thread tells both, and nothing in the text caught a thread that
     told one of the two.
   - *What changed.* The record thread now tells the coordinator "of each wake that it makes, and of its cause". The
     first commit had it tell only of a wake on a cause that it read on main itself. A new sentence gives the
     coordinator its half: "Where a thread has told the coordinator of a cause and no word of the wake follows, the
     coordinator passes that cause to the record thread." A thread that told the record thread alone shows the other
     way round: the coordinator hears of the wake and of its cause from the record thread.
   - *One sentence more, which the cure needs.* A cause can now reach the record thread twice, from the thread and
     from the coordinator. So: "It makes one wake for one cause, however often it is told of it." Without it the cure
     would bring what the third point of G87 warns of, a wake that "comes twice".
   - *What it costs, and why it is worth that.* One line from the record thread to the coordinator for each wake. The
     record thread already sends the coordinator each new entry of the working files word for word, and a line on a
     wake can ride in such a message. It costs the coordinator no message of its own, and it is the coordinator's load
     that the owner's change is to lighten (R32, its point 0).
   - *Why not have a thread tell the record thread alone,* which would need no catching. Rules 1, 6 and 7 have a
     thread tell the coordinator: of a stop, that its PR is ready, that it asks for a verdict. A stop has to reach the
     coordinator, which decides whom it holds, and the coordinator keeps the merge lists. So a thread tells both, as
     the cast has had it since version 4 for the owner's words.
2. *A stop waits for main.* The first commit had the record thread's sending of an entry be what stops a thread, and
   had it send an entry "once it is on main". So a stop waited for a PR, its check and its merge. "Under version 5 no
   stop waited for a merge. If the wait is meant, say it; if not, a sentence."
   - *It was not meant.* The new sentence: "An entry that stops a thread is the exception: it goes to that thread as
     soon as it is written, word for word, and does not wait for main."
   - *Why the record thread sends it, and not the coordinator meanwhile.* One doer for each act (G87, its third
     point). If the coordinator might tell a thread to stop while the entry is on its way, every stopped thread gets
     two messages for one act, and the coordinator spends on stops the very messages that the change is to spare it.
     When the record thread cannot send a stop in good time, the hand-back gives that one to the coordinator, as the
     first commit already had it.
   - *Why "word for word".* Such an entry is not on main yet, so it cannot go "by its number alone".
   - *The entry that lets a thread resume still waits for main,* as it did under version 5: rule 1 has the ruling go
     into rulings.md first. A resume that comes a minute later harms nothing.
3. *The copies.* Two things.
   - *One copy of each book, in the rule itself.* The rule had "the newest copies", and only part 4's row kept "no
     older one" and took the replaced copy away. "Your reason (a commit id does not sort by age) holds for any
     project, and the sister project gets the rule without the row." So the rule now says it itself: the record thread
     "takes away any copy that the new one replaces, so that the place holds one copy of each book and no older one".
     With that, "no one changes it once it is made" became "no one changes it, and no one but the record thread takes
     it away": taking a replaced copy away is now an act of the rule's own, and its one doer is named. Part 4's row
     stays word for word, since it already says the same of Lemuria's folder.
   - *Between a merge and the new copy the place holds the old book,* and the Road Bible has no version in its head to
     tell the two apart. The review thread's way: "the record thread tells it of each new copy by name". The rule now
     has the record thread tell the coordinator "of each copy by its name as soon as it has made it", and a last
     sentence says which copy counts: "After a merge that changes a book, the newest copy of it is the one that the
     record thread has told the coordinator of since that merge, and the coordinator reads no earlier one." The same
     word tells the coordinator of a change to a book that merged without its knowing, such as a certified change to
     the steps and features.
4. *Rule 1's sending to a thread that starts work.* Rule 1 has the entry of a ruling that changes the rules go, "until
   the change merges, to every thread that starts work". By the clause that sending is the record thread's, and only
   the coordinator knows of a start. The sentence on what the coordinator passes on followed the one on a thread's
   cause "for such a wake", and read as speaking of wakes alone. It now begins "A cause for a sending or a wake that
   the coordinator alone knows of", and it names "the start of a thread" among its examples, as the review thread
   proposed.
5. *The bracket.* The review thread asks whether "(that its PR is ready, that a blocker is corrected, that it asks for
   a verdict)" is a list or examples. They are examples, and the bracket now says so: it ends with "for instance". Why
   not a full list: the rules give more causes that a thread tells the coordinator of, a stop that it calls and a map
   that it has updated among them, and a list would have to be kept in step with rules 1, 6 and 7. The sentence around
   the bracket covers them all: whatever cause for such a wake a thread tells the coordinator of, it tells the record
   thread the same.
6. *Rule 6's last resort,* "and the coordinator tells it so". The review thread sorts it as telling a thread what an
   entry of rulings.md says, and so as the record thread's, beside "tells the owning thread" and "passes word of it to
   the janitor". I sort it the same way: what the thread is told there is what the owner's entry says, that no janitor
   can be created, and what rule 6 has follow from it. The text does not change for it. The review thread's report
   gives the sorting sentence by sentence, "so that the record holds it".

**One word more.** "Its own doubt" became "The record thread's own doubt". The new sentence before it has the
coordinator for its subject.
