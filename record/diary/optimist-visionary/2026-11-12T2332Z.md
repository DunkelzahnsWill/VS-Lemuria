**How I read the owner's words, and why so.**
- *A standing rule, and not a sentence among the alignments.* The question that the owner answered names "a design
  rule", and the book's rules of the design are this list, which "every brief keeps". The rule prices, as rules 4 to 6
  do, and it will meet every later brief that has a Mystery do work. Why not beside the table of the nine alignments:
  that section says what the alignments are, and a brief is held against the list.
- *The twelfth, at the end.* The record cites the standing rules by number: rule 7 in the briefs, in my map and in
  many findings, and rule 10 in part 3. So none may move, and the new one takes the next number, though its nearest
  kin are rules 4 to 6.
- *The words are R39's.* The owner calls the coordinator's short form "a slight mis-characterization" and gives the
  fuller one, so where R39 and the short form differ, R39 stands. The rule does not say that the Elementals are "the
  efficient choice": it says that they are basic and simple, and efficient because they map onto the world. It does
  not say that the Mysteries act "beyond" the Elementals, which was R36's word: it says, as R39 does, that they work
  more broadly, within concepts. The fire and the berry bush are the owner's own example, kept as an example, in one
  sentence.
- *Three words of the owner's that the rule leaves out.* The first is "real", of the world: the book says "the world"
  of the game's world throughout. The second is "mechanically": the book keeps that word's kin for the game's
  mechanical power. The third is "IE:". The sentences say the same without them.
- *The price is R36's and R38's.* R36 raises "the charge consumption of all mysteries", so that it is "more efficient
  to use Elementals over Mysteries", and R38 has the hand pay more as well as a machine: "Both machines and
  evocations".
- *No number.* The owner called 4 times "reasonable", as mason's offer. A number in the concept would make every
  change of balance a change to the concept, and balance is step 7's work. The standing rules name no number elsewhere
  either: rule 4 says "far more efficient", and the ten is in the briefs.
- *The rule prices the evocation, and what that leaves out.* The owner's "all mysteries" follows Bloom, which is an
  evocation, and mason's question to the owner was of "a Mystery working". The rule prices a Mystery's evocation,
  "cast by hand or worked by a machine". Those are the briefs' own words for the two: a machine "works these four as
  it works any crystal's evocation". So the rule does not reach a Glint, which is no evocation and by the owner's
  answer burns no more. And it says nothing of what a Mystery crystal gathers, which is standing rule 5's matter.
- *Like with like.* The rule compares an evocation with one "cast or worked the same way at the same Evolution".
  Over-driven, a cast costs twice, and an Amplify doubles a working's cost. Without those words a brief could seem to
  break the rule wherever an over-driven Elemental cast costs more than a plain Mystery one.
- *The last sentence,* "a machine's price stays on the one scale of rule 6". Rule 6 has every action of a machine
  "priced on one scale". The Mysteries' price is one more rule of that scale, and never a second scale. I told mason
  so at 22:56Z, before the owner's answers, and the rule now says it, so that rules 6 and 12 cannot seem to disagree.
- *Rule 4 needs no word.* With R38's answer 4 the hand and a machine both pay more, so a machine stays as far ahead of
  the hand for a Mystery as for an Elemental.
- *The Bloom limit is not in it.* That a plant may Bloom more than once a day within a machine is the brief's to say:
  part 3 places "limits on how often and how strongly machines Bloom" in step 1 "for the brief to settle".
