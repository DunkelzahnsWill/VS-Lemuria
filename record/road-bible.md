# The Road Bible of Lemuria

The central book of the project "Lemuria: The Song of Stone", as the Commandments set it out under "The record"
(record/COMMANDMENTS.txt). Written by the planning thread ("the Optimist Visionary") from the owner's vision. First
written on 2026-11-12, when Lemuria moved onto the Commandments with the Precursor steps already built.

It has four parts: the concept, the prototype's terms, the steps and features, and the shared list. An "R" with a
number cites an entry of record/rulings.md, and a "G" an entry of record/findings.md. Lemuria was begun before the
Commandments took force in it, which they did on 2026-11-12 at 03:00Z (R19). The old paper is what was written for
Lemuria before then and is named in part 4. The old paper is history: where it and this book differ, this book stands
(R17).


## Part 1. The concept

### What Lemuria is

**Lemuria: The Song of Stone** is a crystal-and-stone tech mod for the game Vintage Story, version 1.22.7. Its mod id
is `lemuria`. The name and the subtitle are the owner's (2026-10-26).

You find strange glowing crystals, farm them like crops, refine them into eight alignments in four twin pairs, raise
them from Glittering to Baleful, and spend their charge: first by hand in naked evocations, then in machines of
living sparkstone that learn what you teach them, carry you in hovering and flying stone vehicles, mend themselves
and at last raise engine citadels.

The mod began from an idea that the owner parked on 2026-10-22. The owner's scratchpad (part 4) holds it, entered
there as the owner's words of that day, 20:53Z:

> an "Atlantis: The Lost Empire" inspired tech mod, focusing on the growth of crystals and the shaping of stone (maybe
> living or crystal-infused stone?). No GUI; indications should be immersive and natural (lights or symbols on the
> models and tools themselves, similar to Botania). Scope: exploration, into machines, into spell-like or magic-like
> technologies, in the vein of Atlantis, Castle in the Sky, or Escaflowne. Look into movable or "flying" block
> structures.

That is where Lemuria began, and it is given here as history. What has been settled since stands in the rest of this
part, and where the two differ, the rest stands: the Journal is the one screen (standing rule 1), and multi-block
flying structures are shelved (below).

Three films give the look and the arc, and nothing else: *Atlantis: The Lost Empire* (crystals that hold power and
light up glyphs), *Castle in the Sky* (a floating stone island run by a crystal) and *Escaflowne* (crystal-powered
machines and fliers). The mod borrows their mood only, with no character names, logos or likenesses.

### The crystal cycle

1. **Find.** Breaking the crystals of a vug, a hollow pocket of the game's own crystals underground, gives a small
   chance of a *strange glowing crystal*: an Unaligned crystal, and the start of everything. Ruins hold a few
   *precursor crystals*, already aligned and evolved by those who came before. They shorten the chain and nothing
   more.
2. **Farm.** Crystals grow like crops. An Unaligned crystal is *ground* in the game's quern into *seedlings* of its
   own Evolution, and a seedling set in a *cradle* passes through five *growth stages* into a crystal you can harvest.
3. **Refine.** An Unaligned crystal takes an *alignment* from the environment it is refined in. At low Evolution
   refining can be sloppy. Each higher Evolution asks for a purer environment.
4. **Evolve.** A crystal's *Evolution* rises in an *evolution ring*: aligned crystals of one Evolution stand around a
   cradle while an Unaligned seedling of that Evolution grows in it, and the harvest comes out one Evolution higher.
   An aligned crystal is refined at the Evolution of the Unaligned crystal it came from, never higher, so refining
   Unaligned crystals gates everything else.
5. **Charge.** A crystal charges from its alignment's environment. Higher Evolutions are pickier, and the top ones
   need environments you engineer with special tools.
6. **Work.** Each alignment has one *naked evocation*, its single use by hand. It is wasteful on purpose: it
   bootstraps you into machines.
7. **Build.** Machines are built of *sparkstone* that holds crystals. Charge moves through touching stone, and from
   the mid-game it also travels in *beams* along a line of sight.

The word *precursor* has three uses in this project, and the word beside it tells them apart. *Precursor crystals*
and *precursor ruins* are things of the mod, left by those who came before, and the only use a player meets. The
*Precursor steps* (part 3) and *the precursor*, the thread that keeps the old paper (part 4), are words of the record
and name nothing in the mod.

### The nine alignments

Every crystal has one of nine alignments: eight in four twin pairs, each the inversion of its twin, and Unaligned at
the centre with no twin, *the Hidden Fifth*, the feedstock that all the others are refined from.

| Family | Alignment | Twin | Refined in | Naked evocation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Four Elementals | Fire | Ice | Heat: fires, firepits, forges, open lava, hot climates | Ignite: starts a fire at range |
| | Ice | Fire | Cold: frost, snow and ice, cold climates | Quench: puts out fires and freezes water at range |
| | Earth | Sky | Depth: far below the surface, walled in stone | Tremor: raises a pillar or mound |
| | Sky | Earth | Open sky: height, open air overhead, wind, lightning and rain, but not sunlight | Gale: a gust that pushes creatures and loose items away |
| The Four Mysteries | Life | Death | Light and living things | Bloom: moves a plant or crop on by a growth stage |
| | Death | Life | Darkness, bones, creatures dying nearby | Wither: withers plants, and from Glowing drains creatures and players |
| | Time | Rust | High temporal stability, with temporal gears near | Rewind: turns food back toward fresh |
| | Rust | Time | Low temporal stability, temporal storms, decay | Progress: moves slow changes on, so cheese ripens, meat cures and food rots for compost |
| The Hidden Fifth | Unaligned | none | Not refined: ground into seedlings and farmed in any cradle | Command: teaches or commands sparkstone |

A Mystery reads a condition of the place (light, darkness, high or low temporal stability) times a *sign* that the
player brings beside it (living things, bones, temporal gears, rot and rusty gears), so it wakes only where its sign
is set (the owner, 2026-11-09).

### Evolution, and the danger that climbs with it

*Evolution* is what a crystal can do, in five stages: Glittering, Glowing, Luminous, Radiant and Baleful. At the
bottom a crystal does only what its alignment means most directly. Higher up it reaches further and more indirectly,
and it is more dangerous to keep.

Twins pull against each other. In a cradle's environment they cancel. Twins in range of each other, or in one
container, are *dialoguing*, in the owner's word, and what a dialogue does climbs with Evolution:

- **Glittering:** the twins *neutralize*. Both turn Unaligned.
- **Glowing:** they *discharge* harmlessly, and now and then one of them *cracks*.
- **Luminous:** they *react*, harming creatures nearby or having light effects on the world around: small fires,
  frozen water, crops killed.
- **Radiant:** the reaction is truly dangerous, and what it does to the world stays until a *clean-up*.
- **Baleful:** the reaction is a meltdown, a *Bale storm* in the owner's words. The crystals do not survive, it may
  leave *scars* on the world, and its clean-up is long and expensive, with dedicated tools.

A crack is one step of *stress*, which over-driving a crystal causes too. A cracked crystal still works, its third
crack shatters it, and it is *repaired* by resting in a cradle. *Crystal jars* and *crystal racks* keep crystals out
of dialogues up to Glowing. From Luminous up only a *shielded jar* holds a crystal back.

From Luminous up an unshielded crystal is also a danger to whoever holds it, carries it or stands near it, in its
alignment's nature. That is its *exposure*: weakest at Luminous, stronger at Radiant, and in full only at Baleful. So
players get used to stricter safety, and gather the protective wearables (*PPE*) such power asks for, as they climb,
never thrown in at the deep end. The owner calls the players who handle such crystals magical nuclear engineers.

### Sparkstone and machines

*Sparkstone* is crystal-infused stone, the mod's living stone. It has three tiers, and each adds an ability:

| Sparkstone tier | What the stone adds |
| --- | --- |
| Awakened | Carries charge by touch, glows and hums |
| Ascendant | *Heals*: regrows blocks taken out of its own machine, only into empty space, paid in charge, and never inside another player's claim while the claims switch is on (standing rule 7) |
| Sovereign | *Reshapes*: builds a structure it was taught, such as the *engine citadels* that large or very powerful works need to function |

A sparkstone block's room for crystals is its *Complexity Rating*: lower stone has less room, and a crystal takes
space by its Evolution.

Machines speak the *Crystal Vocabulary*. The *Basic Vocabulary* is the verbs every player learns, each a form carved
into Awakened sparkstone: Gather, Refine, Focus, Scatter and Amplify, with the Crystal Motor beside them, which
drives the game's own mechanical power. The *Hidden Vocabulary* is further verbs found only through odd experiments
or builds, niche but powerful. A *module* is a part fitted to a machine or a stone vehicle that changes or extends
what it does. A *duet* is two aligned crystals of different alignments working as one, to make what neither makes
alone.

Stone learns by *teaching*: the player toggles blocks with special tools, within a radius that grows with the
stone's tier. A *Runic Tablet* carries a learned task or structure to another machine. Unaligned crystals, whose
evocation is Command, give stone its orders.

### By hand, by vehicle, by book

A naked evocation is an alignment's one working by hand, so no wheel or gesture is needed to choose one. Any crystal,
in the hand or in a machine, can be *over-driven* for a stronger effect at the cost of stress.

*Stone vehicles* are carved from shaped stone and driven by a crystal, in two forms: a fast single rider, and a slow
carrier for cargo, livestock or other players. Both hover close to the ground at first, and modules make them truly
fly.

*The Pilgrim's Journal* is the mod's guide book and its one GUI: a book screen of its own, with chapters that open as
the player finds, grows and builds, and hints at the Hidden Vocabulary. Lemuria's items stay out of the game's
handbook, so the Journal is where they are learned. Everything else is read from the world through *indicators*:
glow, glyph light, particles, sound, a crystal's size, its cracks. Sound is a main indicator, as the subtitle
promises: crystals and sparkstone hum louder as their charge rises and chime when it changes level, each alignment
hums its own note, and twins about to dialogue lose their notes to a discordant hum.

### The standing rules of the design

These are the owner's, settled before this book, and every brief keeps them. One sentence among them is new with this
book and says so: the last of rule 3.

1. **No GUI but the Journal.** Charge is shown by glow and hum, never by a number.
2. **Four scales, four sets of words,** so that no two can be mixed up: the growth stage (five of them); the
   Evolution (Glittering, Glowing, Luminous, Radiant, Baleful); the sparkstone tier (Awakened, Ascendant, Sovereign);
   and the charge level (empty, low, high, full).
3. **One meaning to a word.** Each word of the mod has one meaning, in code, in the record and in chat. The old
   glossary lists the words as they stood (part 4, in the pre-production document at its revision 247), and this book
   uses them in that meaning. New with this book: from here on a word's meaning is settled in the record, in this book
   or in the brief of the feature it belongs to.
4. **Naked evocation is wasteful on purpose.** A machine doing the same work is far more efficient.
5. **The energy line** (the owner, 2026-11-02). At the same Evolution, each newer source of charge brings more on
   average than the one before it, so no later invention is the worse choice. Stores make no charge and stay off the
   line.
6. **One Actions-to-Charge scale** (the owner, 2026-10-28). Every action a machine does is priced on one scale, for a
   consistent economy of action.
7. **One claims switch.** Every land claim rule in Lemuria goes through one switch, so that none can drift (the owner,
   2026-10-27). With it on, as it is by default, Lemuria's workings spare what lies inside another player's claim, and
   only a Bale storm's scars do not. A server admin can turn it off in game, and every feature reads the same switch.
   With it off, Lemuria's workings no longer spare a claim, and every Lemuria block that is used by hand also opens
   inside another player's claim (R9). The game's own breaking and placing of blocks inside another player's claim
   stay as the game rules them, whatever the switch says: the owner's words open Lemuria's blocks there to use and to
   nothing more (the card of 2026-11-10, and R9).
8. **Multiplayer-safe from the first line.** The server holds the state and sends it to clients through the game's
   own channels, and nothing works only in single player. Server time, network traffic and the size of the zip stay
   small.
9. **Harmony only where it adds a capability the game's API lacks,** and never as a prefix that cancels another
   mod's patch. Lemuria leans on the systems the game already has (its quern, its chutes, its charcoal pit, its
   lightning, its temporal stability, its claims) and stays compatible with other mods.
10. **Testing.** The owner tests in single player until release, and multiplayer is tested once, before v1.0. Art is
    drawn by scripts and judged by the owner in play.
11. **The owner publishes.** The repository is public and the licence is MIT. The mod is released on ModDB when it is
    ready, by the owner.

### What Lemuria is not

- Not a power-grid mod with cables and meters. Charge travels the way stone and light would carry it.
- Not a replacement for the game's mechanical power. The Crystal Motor drives it.
- Not multi-block flying ships. The owner shelved multi-block flying structures on 2026-10-26, and stone vehicles
  stand in their place.
- Not a mod of screens and numbers. The Journal is the one screen.
- Not a copy of the films it looks to.

Out of scope for v1.0: multi-block flying structures, and new creatures, so a Bale storm's large beasts are the game's
own.

## Part 3. The steps and features

### How Lemuria counts features

A feature is one part of the mod with a folder of its own in the code. The repo has been laid out that way since its
first step (README, "Working in parallel"), and the old notes use the word that way. Each feature has:

- **a number**, which is never changed and never reused;
- **a short name**, one lowercase word, which names its folders and its brief, `record/briefs/<short name>.md`. It is
  a handle for the code and the record, not a name the player reads: it may be changed, by a change to this book,
  until the feature's brief is put up, and from then on it stays, whatever the thing itself comes to be called;
- **its owning thread**, which for every feature in this book is the builder **mason** (R5, R15);
- **its folders**, which are the ones named for it: `Lemuria/<Short name>/` for its code, written with a capital
  first letter (`Lemuria/Crystals/` for `crystals`); every folder named `<short name>` under
  `Lemuria/assets/lemuria/` (such as `lang/crystals/` and `shapes/block/crystals/`); every file
  `Lemuria/assets/lemuria/patches/<short name>-*.json`; and every file `tests/smoke/<short name>.*`.

A feature that is not built yet has no folders on main. They come with its first code, named as above. Within a step
the features are numbered, not ordered: the order of building is mason's own plan, in its map (rule 5).

### Where features meet

Lemuria's features reach into each other. The Mysteries put their alignments into the crystals' folders, a machine's
secret lies with the machine, and every old step from old step 1 on wrote into the Journal's folders. So this book
says whose such work is, and what comes before it.

1. **The folder decides.** What lies in a feature's folders is that feature's, whichever feature's work brings it
   there, and that feature's brief describes it. The tests of rule 7 are held against that brief.
2. **The briefs come first.** A brief names the existing parts its feature touches (rule 2). Where the feature's work
   would add to or change what lies in another feature's folders, that feature's brief is changed first, the way a
   settled brief is changed (rule 4), so that it says what its folders will hold. No code for a later feature goes
   into a feature's folders before that feature's own brief is settled and describes it (rule 2). So nothing is
   written in the folders of features 1 to 9 for a later feature before the feature concerned has its first brief (R3)
   and that brief is changed for what is added. Such changes may ride in the pull request that carries the new
   feature's brief.
3. **The code may ride with the feature.** A pull request that carries a feature changes that feature's own folders
   and, where its brief says so, the shared files of part 4. What the feature needs changed in the folders of another
   feature that its thread owns or has been handed follows the change to that feature's brief (point 2). Once that
   change is merged, the code may ride in the pull request that carries the feature, and that pull request's
   description names each other feature it changes and the merged change to that feature's brief (rule 4). The owner
   ruled so in R24, in answer to R23, and R25 confirms how R24 is read. Such code may also go on its own, as a patch
   to the feature in whose folders it lies: its verdict is then "out", and it waits for the owner's merge (rule 4).
4. **A later fix** to any of it is a patch to the feature in whose folders it lies.
